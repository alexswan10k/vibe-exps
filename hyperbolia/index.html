The honest two-dimensional version. Every tile is provably identical — same side length, same angle — yet seven of them meet at each corner, where flat space allows only six. The orange line is a geodesic: it never touches the rim, because the rim is infinitely far away.

The rooms never move. This switches how distance is measured — and so how the corridor fades. In hyperbolic space a step is cheap: the same lattice distance costs less and less, so the walk goes on long after flat space would have ground to a halt.

HYPERBOLIA

an infinite architecture, and the geometry behind it

Euclidean cornerα + β + γ = π
Hyperbolic cornerα + β + γ < π

A corner holds less than a right angle. Six rooms can no longer close up around you — six doorways open into six more rooms, and those need six more again. A space with corners like that can hold infinitely many rooms and still never bring you back where you started.

You are standing in one. Through every doorway is another room identical to this one, and through those, more — receding toward a point you can approach but never reach. Walk for as long as you like: the view will not change, and it will not end.
